# The rounds after the first draft

The first draft is a third of the work. Then the reviewers read it, comment in writing, and the
page changes shape. This file covers that stretch: what their questions do to the page, how a
theme leaves the moves, how he reviews a restructure, and how to change the page without losing
anyone's comments.

## Read first

1. Fetch the page with its discussions. Read every comment, every reply of his, and the
   reactions. A reaction is not a comment, but it shows what a reviewer agreed with or worried
   about.
2. Read what he wrote back. His replies are decisions, and they are his own sentences on exactly
   the points that were weak. Most of the next draft can be lifted from them.
3. Read what was said about the page elsewhere since: the review session, the chat thread with
   the reviewers. A reviewer may have taken a topic off the table there.
4. List the comments with no reply yet. He will want a sentence for each.

## The questions, and what each does to the page

These came back in writing once reviewers had read a full draft. Use them on every move before
he is asked.

| The question | What it means | What to do |
|---|---|---|
| What stops the market leader from doing the same? | An advantage is claimed and not earned. | Give a structural reason in his words, or drop the claim and say why the work is needed. He may decide a move needs no such answer. That is his call, and it closes the point. |
| What does this sentence mean? | The line is abstract. | Replace it with the mechanism: what happens, in what order, and who gets what. |
| How is this different from today? | The move repeats last year's recap. | Take the phrase out of the recap, and open the move with what is still done by hand. |
| What happens if we skip it next year? | The theme may not be a move. | If the honest answer is "little", move it to an appendix for future consideration. |
| Is this direction, or a feature we simply add? How big is it? | The theme is small and bounded. | Move it to an appendix as a functionality. Have the last estimate ready: tickets, weeks, people. |
| Is the order the priority? | The most important move sits low. | Put the moves in the order he would defend, the foundation first. |
| Is this move only a fix? | It reads as defensive. | Add what the fix lets the product become. |
| Whose bet is this? | Another squad owns part of it. | One sentence on who decides what. |

A reviewer who says "keep this out for now" has decided. Record it, remove the topic, and do not
bring it back in a later draft.

## Moving a theme out of the moves

1. Agree the new status with him in one line: "for future consideration, not part of this North
   Star", or "a functionality we add", with for whom and how far.
2. Keep the text. Move it under a collapsed appendix heading with the status line on top.
3. Take year words out of it. It is no longer a commitment for that year.
4. If one sentence of the idea supports another move, keep that sentence there and nothing more.
5. Leave it out of the remaining moves unless he says otherwise. The point was to get it out.
6. Then run the fallout checklist below.

## Fallout checklist after a reorder or a cut

A changed order breaks sentences far from the change. Check each of these:

- the hand-off sentence, which names the first move;
- any first-person sentence that justifies the old order;
- the closing list, including a last line that began "While doing the above";
- cross-references inside the moves ("the model from point 1 above");
- the evidence endings ("Backs move n");
- the recap, if a move now opens differently;
- the story slide and any deck built from it;
- the review copy and the notes in memory.

Say which sentences you removed because of the reorder. He may want one back.

## The review copy

For a restructure he reviews a local file first. The page changes only after that.

- Put a legend at the top:
  - plain text: his page text, moved or untouched;
  - **bold**: his own words from replies to reviewers and from his notes, cleaned and placed;
  - ***bold italic***: yours, to confirm or cut.
- Under the legend, list the sentences that were removed, and log each pass in one line as his
  answers come in ("third pass, after your answers: ...").
- Keep the list of your own sentences current. When the page is finally updated, the report
  names every sentence of yours that is now on it.
- When he rewrites one of your sentences, it becomes his. Change its mark and drop it from your
  list.
- After the page is updated, keep the file in step with it, and say at the top that it now
  mirrors the page.

## Keeping comment threads through a rewrite

Reviewers resolve their own threads. If a rewrite drops them, their feedback looks ignored and
he loses the record of what was answered. In Notion the threads can be carried, even through a
full replacement of the page:

1. Fetch the page with discussions included. Commented text comes back wrapped in a span that
   carries the discussion URL.
2. In the new content, wrap the text that should carry each thread in the same span. The thread
   re-attaches by its discussion id, in a different block or a new one, on a heading or a toggle
   title as well.
3. A thread whose sentence is being deleted goes on the sentence that replaces it, so the
   reviewer can still read the answer and resolve it.
4. Try it once on the least important thread with a small targeted edit. Then do the full
   replacement.
5. Check afterwards: every thread is listed, still inline, still open, and the reactions are
   still on their text. Page history can undo a bad write.

Text that loses its span loses the thread. A small edit inside a commented paragraph is safe when
it replaces a few words and leaves the span around them.

## After each round

- Report what changed, what did not, which sentences of yours are on the page, and where each
  comment thread now sits.
- List what is still open with the reviewers: comments without a reply, a question about size or
  time, links they cannot open.
- Update the slide in the same pass, and say if a deck version is now behind.
